There are 60 empty boxes $B_{1}, \ldots, B_{60}$ in a row on a table and an unlimited supply of pebbles. Given a positive integer $n$, Alice and Bob play the following game. In the first round, Alice takes $n$ pebbles and distributes them into the 60 boxes as she wishes. Each subsequent round consists of two steps: (a) Bob chooses an integer $k$ with $1 \leqslant k \leqslant 59$ and splits the boxes into the two groups $B_{1}, \ldots, B_{k}$ and $B_{k+1}, \ldots, B_{60}$. (b) Alice picks one of these two groups, adds one pebble to each box in that group, and removes one pebble from each box in the other group. 模型抽取答案\boxed{1830}
归一化1830
正确答案960
查看模型完整作答
To solve this problem, we can analyze the game by looking at the prefix sums of the pebbles in the boxes. 

Let $m = 60$ be the number of boxes. Let $a_i$ be the number of pebbles in box $B_i$ at the end of a round. We want to ensure that $a_i \ge 1$ for all $i$ at the end of every round. 

Consider the prefix sums $P_j = \sum_{i=1}^j a_i$ for $j = 0, 1, \ldots, m$, with $P_0 = 0$. The condition $a_i \ge 1$ is equivalent to $P_j - P_{j-1} \ge 1$, which means the sequence $P_j$ must be strictly increasing. 

To simplify this, we can introduce a new sequence $Q_j = P_j - j$. The condition that $P_j$ is strictly increasing becomes $Q_j - Q_{j-1} \ge 0$, meaning the sequence $Q_0, Q_1, \ldots, Q_m$ must be non-decreasing. Initially, $Q_0 = 0$ and $Q_m = P_m - m = n - m$. 

In each round, Bob chooses an integer $k \in \{1, \ldots, m-1\}$, splitting the boxes into two groups: $L = \{1, \ldots, k\}$ and $R = \{k+1, \ldots, m\}$. Alice then chooses a sign $c \in \{+1, -1\}$. If $c = 1$, she adds 1 pebble to each box in $L$ and removes 1 from each in $R$. If $c = -1$, she does the opposite.

The change in the prefix sum $P_j$ is given by the function:
$$ f(j, k) = \begin{cases} 
c \cdot j & \text{if } j \le k \\ 
c \cdot (2k - j) & \text{if } j > k 
\end{cases} $$
Thus, the new sequence becomes $Q'_j = Q_j + c \cdot f(j, k)$. Alice's goal is to choose $c$ such that the sequence $Q'$ remains non-decreasing.

Notice that the function $f(j, k)$ increases by 1 for each step up to $k$, and then decreases by 1 for each step after $k$. This shape acts like a "mountain" or "valley" added to $Q$. If Alice chooses $c = 1$, $Q$ gets a mountain added to it; if she chooses $c = -1$, $Q$ gets a valley.
To ensure $Q'$ is non-decreasing, the "slopes" must not cause any decreases. It turns out that Alice can maintain the non-decreasing property indefinitely if and only if the maximum possible "depth" she might be forced to create does not exceed the initial headroom of the sequence.

$$3(2-x) - x > 2x$$

$$6x < 6$$
$$x < 1$$

**情况 2：当 $0 \le x \le 2$ 时**

$$3(x-2) + x > 2x$$
$$4x - 6 > 2x$$
$$2x > 6$$

To find the expression for the cyclotron frequency of the electron as a function of time in the ultrarelativistic limit, we start by analyzing the motion of the charged particle in the uniform magnetic field.

1. **Relativistic Momentum and Radius:**
For a relativistic particle, the cyclotron radius $r$ is given by equating the magnetic force to the centripetal force:
$$e v B = \frac{\gamma m v^2}{r}$$
$$r = \frac{\gamma m v}{e B} = \frac{p}{e B}$$
In the ultrarelativistic limit, the speed of the particle $v$ approaches the speed of light $c$ ($v \approx c$). Therefore, its momentum $p$ can be approximated by its energy $E$:
$$p \approx \frac{E}{c}$$
Substituting this into the expression for the radius gives:
$$r = \frac{E}{e B c}$$

2. **Cyclotron Frequency:**
The cyclotron frequency $f$ (ordinary frequency, not angular frequency) is defined as the number of revolutions per unit time, which is the speed of the particle divided by the circumference of its circular path:
$$f = \frac{v}{2 \pi r} \approx \frac{c}{2 \pi r}$$
Substituting the expression for $r$ into this equation, we obtain:
$$f = \frac{c}{2 \pi \left( \frac{E}{e B c} \right)} = \frac{e B c^2}{2 \pi E}$$
