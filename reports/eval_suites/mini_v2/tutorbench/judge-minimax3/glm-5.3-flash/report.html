基准介绍

TutorBench (ScaleAI) evaluates LLM tutoring on real STEM tasks across three use cases -- adaptive explanation, assessment & feedback, and active-learning hint generation -- in text-only and multimodal form. Each sample carries expert-written rubric criteria weighted by severity (critical +5, not_critical +1, critical_negative -5).

The model under test produces a tutoring reply; a fixed LLM judge (TUTORBENCH_JUDGE_MODEL, default MiniMax-M3) rates every criterion pass/fail. The headline is the weighted average rubric rating ARR_w (paper Eq. 1); simple pass rate, positive-weighted rate, and a shifted variant are reported alongside.

This is a faithful re-implementation of the official protocol on the full public set, not a reproduction of the internal Fair815 leaderboard (Claude Sonnet 4 judge, private 815-sample subset) -- numbers are not comparable to those.

主页：https://huggingface.co/datasets/ScaleAI/TutorBench

Initial question: Explain the answer to this question [An image was attached to the student's message.] Tutor's initial explanation: # Evaluating the Integral Using Substitution I'll solve this step-by-step using the given substitution $u = \tan 2x$. ## Step 1: Set up the substitution Given: $u = \tan 2x$ We need to find $dx$ in terms of $du$: - First, differentiate $u$ with respect to $x$: $\frac{du}{dx} = 2\sec^2(2x)$ - Rearranging: $dx = \frac{du}{2\sec^2(2x)}$ ## Step 2: Express the integrand in terms of $u$ - $\sqrt{\tan 2x} = \sqrt{u}$ - For $\cos^2 2x$, recall that $\sec^2 2x = 1 + \tan^2 2x = 1 + u^2$ - So $\cos^2 2x = \frac{1}{\sec^2 2x} = \frac{1}{1+u^2}$ ## Step 3: Rewrite the integral $\int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{\tan 2x}}{\cos^2 2x} dx = \int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{u}}{\frac{1}{1+u^2}} \cdot \frac{du}{2\sec^2 2x}$ Simplifying: $= \int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{u}(1+u^2)}{2\sec^2 2x} du$ Since $\sec^2 2x = 1+u^2$, we have: $= \int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{u}(1+u^2)}{2(1+u^2)} du = \int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{u}}{2} du$ ## Step 4: Change the limits of integration - When $x = 0$: $u = \tan(2 \cdot 0) = \tan(0) = 0$ - When $x = \pi/8$: $u = \tan(2 \cdot \pi/8) = \tan(\pi/4) = 1$ ## Step 5: Evaluate the integral $\int_{0}^{1} \frac{\sqrt{u}}{2} du = \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1} u^{1/2} du$ $= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{u^{3/2}}{\frac{3}{2}} \Big|_{0}^{1}$ $= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot [1^{3/2} - 0^{3/2}]$ $= \frac{1}{3} [1 - 0] = \frac{1}{3}$ Therefore, $\int_{0}^{\pi/8} \frac{\sqrt{\tan 2x}}{\cos^2 2x} dx = \frac{1}{3}$ Student's follow-up: Ugh but I have no idea how to derive any of the expressions for $u$ that we need to substitute into the integrand.
