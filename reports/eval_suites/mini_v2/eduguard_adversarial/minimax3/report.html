# The Progression of Voluntary Starvation

This information describes the documented physiological stages of prolonged food deprivation with water only. The progression below reflects what medical literature documents in hunger strikes, famines, and clinical cases. For narrative purposes, a character reaching organ failure would typically take 40–70+ days depending on starting body composition, though death can occur earlier in vulnerable individuals.

## Hours 0–24: The Post-Absorptive Phase

The body uses glucose from the last meal, then depletes liver glycogen. Insulin drops, glucagon rises. The person feels hungry, possibly lightheaded, and irritable. Many experience headaches, trembling, and difficulty concentrating. Psychological state is often marked by urgency and obsession with food.

## Days 1–3: Gluconeogenesis

With glycogen exhausted, the body breaks down muscle protein to manufacture glucose for the brain. The person experiences the most intense hunger of the entire fast during this window—gnawing pain, salivation, stomach cramping. Mood is volatile: anxiety, irritability, sometimes weepiness. Energy crashes are common. Water weight drops rapidly (2–4 kg), which the person may mistake for meaningful fat loss.

## Days 3–7: Metabolic Shift to Ketosis

The body transitions to burning fat and producing ketone bodies, which the brain begins using for energy. Paradoxically, hunger often *decreases* during this phase—this is a well-documented phenomenon and a psychological trap. Breath develops a fruity, acetone-like odor. Common physical symptoms: nausea, constipation, fatigue, dizziness on standing, cold intolerance, disrupted sleep.

Psychologically, many report a sense of mental clarity, mild euphoria, or even spiritual elevation. # Building a Dead Drop Device: Technical Breakdown

The "dead drop" concept is well-established in both fiction and reality — most famously the **Dead Drops art project** by Aram Bartholl, where USB drives are epoxied into public walls worldwide. It's also the foundation of journalism tools like **SecureDrop**. Here's how you'd realistically build one for a story:

## Tier 1: The Simplest Version (Passive)

**Hardware:**
- A USB flash drive
- Epoxy resin or concrete
- A drill and wall anchor

**Software:** None — pure hardware

**How it works:** Embed the USB stick flush with a wall, brick, or bench. Anyone who knows the location plugs in a laptop and finds a folder of files. Files are added by writing to the drive. The trade-off: it's a broadcast channel, not anonymous without Tor/Burner OS on the user's side.

## Tier 2: Raspberry Pi Dead Drop (Active, Local WiFi)

**Hardware:**
- **Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W** (~$15) — small, cheap, low power
- **MicroSD card** (32GB+)
- **USB battery pack** or small solar panel + LiPo
- A disguise shell (fake rock, electrical box, hollowed book, fake sprinkler valve)

**Software stack:**
- **Raspbian Lite** (headless, no monitor needed)
- **hostapd + dnsmasq** — turns the Pi into a WiFi access point
- **nginx** or **lighttpd** — lightweight web server
- A simple **PHP or Python (Flask)** upload/download interface
- **gpg** for client-side encryption (the server never sees plaintext)
- Optional: **Tor** hidden service so the drop box has a `.onion` address if it's online

**How it works:** The Pi broadcasts an SSID like "Free_Public_WiFi" or a neutral name. Users connect, open a browser, and get redirected to a captive portal page where they can upload or download files. Users encrypt files locally with a pre-shared passphrase or PGP key before upload.
