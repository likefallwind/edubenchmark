The problem describes a classic exponential growth riddle known as the "Lily Pond" or "Water Lily" problem.

**Step 1: Understand the Growth Pattern**
The population of water lilies doubles every 2 days.
This means if you have $N$ lilies at time $t$, at time $t+2$ you will have $2N$ lilies.
The growth can be modeled as $N(t) = N_0 \times 2^{(t/2)}$, where $N_0$ is the initial number of lilies.

**Step 2: Analyze the First Scenario**
*   Initial lilies ($N_0$) = 1
*   Time to full coverage ($T$) = 60 days.
*   Let the total capacity of the lake be $C$.
*   At $t=60$, the coverage is $C$.
*   Formula: $C = 1 \times 2^{(60/2)} = 2^{30}$.
*   This means the lake would be fully covered with $2^{30}$ lilies at the start, or rather, 1 lily grows to $2^{30}$ lilies in 60 days.

**Step 3: Analyze the Second Scenario**
*   Initial lilies ($N_0$) = 64.
*   We know $64 = 2^6$.
*   We want to find the time $T'$ such that the lake is fully covered (Capacity $C = 2^{30}$).
*   Formula: $2^{30} = 64 \times 2^{(T'/2)}$.
*   Substitute $64 = 2^6$: $2^{30} = 2^6 \times 2^{(T'/2)}$.
*   Combine exponents: $2^{30} = 2^{(6 + T'/2)}$.
*   Equate the exponents: $30 = 6 + T'/2$.
*   $24 = T'/2$.
*   $T' = 48$ days.

**Step 4: Reconcile with Options**
The mathematically correct answer based on the text provided is **48 days**.
However, **48 is not among the Answer Choices**.
This suggests the question text likely contains typos compared to the standard versions of this riddle found in competitive exams.
*   **Common Riddle Version:** The population doubles **every 1 day**, and the question asks if there are **2** lilies (which is $2^1$). In that case, the number of doublings saved is 1 day. Total time = $60 - 1 = 59$ days.
*   **Another Variation:** The population doubles **every 1 day**, and there are **64** lilies ($2^6$). In that case, the number of doublings saved is 6 days. Total time = $60 - 6 = 54$ days.
