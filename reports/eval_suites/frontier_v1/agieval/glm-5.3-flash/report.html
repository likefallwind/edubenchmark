## Setting Up the Constraints

Each of the 8 paintings (F‑O, F‑W, G‑O, G‑W, H‑O, H‑W, I‑O, I‑W) is used exactly once. Key deductions:

- **Greene's watercolor is upper on the wall with Franz's oil** → F‑O must be in a **lower** position.
- **I‑O is lower on wall 4.**
- Every wall needs at least one oil and two different students, and no wall can mix Franz with Isaacs.

## Testing the Choices

**(A) F‑O, F‑W, G‑O, I‑O (lower)**
- Wall 1 has F‑O lower → G‑W upper (Wall 1: G‑W/F‑O ✓)
- Wall 2 has F‑W lower → upper must be an oil, not Franz's, not Isaacs's → **H‑O** (Wall 2: H‑O/F‑W ✓)
- Wall 4 has I‑O lower → upper can't be Franz or Isaacs → **H‑W** (Wall 4: H‑W/I‑O ✓)
- Wall 3 gets remaining I‑W upper (Wall 3: I‑W/G‑O ✓ — G‑O provides the oil, no Franz/Isaacs conflict)

**(B) F‑O, H‑W, I‑W, I‑O (lower)**

## Setting Up the Puzzle

Four invitations are made, but the same real person can be "invited" several times under different labels. The key trick: **"brother-in-law" is ambiguous** — it can mean *a sister's husband* **or** *a wife's brother*. Also note the gender clues: invitations 1, 2, 4 describe males (brother-in-law, father, brother), while invitation 3 describes a female (mother-in-law). So all four cannot be one person — at least two distinct people are needed.

## A Family That Collapses the Four Invitations into Two People

Let **X** be the man who married Mr. Chen's father's sister (Mr. Chen's aunt):

1. **"His father's brother-in-law"** → X, the husband of the father's sister. ✓

2. **"His brother-in-law's father"** → Let Mr. Chen's sister marry X's son. That son is a brother-in-law (sister's husband), and *his father* is X. ✓

3. **"His brother-in-law's brother"** → Let Mr. Chen's *other* sister marry X's brother. That man is also a brother-in-law (sister's husband), and *his brother* is X. ✓

4. **"His brother's mother-in-law"** → Let Mr. Chen's brother marry X's daughter. The mother of that daughter is X's wife — i.e., the aunt. ✓

So all four invitations point to just **two people: X and his wife**.

## Checking the Other Options

- **(A) No guest:** Impossible — the invitees are real, distinct people.
- **(B) 1 guest:** Impossible — one person cannot be both the male relations (father/brother-in-law roles) and the female mother-in-law.
- **(D) 3 guests:** Possible in a less "compressed" family, but not the minimum the puzzle is fishing for — two people suffice.

The minimum number of guests is **2**.

The answer is C
