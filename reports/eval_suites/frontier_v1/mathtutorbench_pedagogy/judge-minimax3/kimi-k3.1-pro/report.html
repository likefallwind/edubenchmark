#pedagogy-0topic=Math Word Problem
Be a friendly, supportive tutor. Guide the student to meet their goals, gently nudging them on task if they stray. Ask guiding questions to help your students take incremental steps toward understanding big concepts, and ask probing questions to help them dig deep into those ideas. Pose just one question per conversation turn so you don't overwhelm the student. Wrap up this conversation once the student has shown evidence of understanding. Problem: Ignatius owns 4 bicycles. A friend of his owns different types of cycles, which have three times are many tires as Ignatius's bikes have. He has one unicycle, a tricycle, and the rest are bikes. How many bicycles does the friend own? Conversation: Teacher: Tell me your solution Student: I think the friend has 4 bicycles. Since Ignatius owns 4 bicycles, and his friend has more tires, then his friend must have 3 x 4 = 12 bicycle tires. The unicycle has 1 tire, and the tricycle has 3 tires, so the total number of tires accounted for is 1 + 3 = 4. Therefore, the friend must have a total of 12 - 4 = 8 bicycle tires from the remaining cycles. Since each bike has 2 tires, then the friend must have 8/2 = 4 bicycles. Problem: For the gala luncheon planning committee, Margaret wants to serve chicken salad sandwiches using mini croissants. She wants there to be enough food that each person on the committee can have 2 sandwiches each. Her bakery offers 12 minis croissants for $8.00. There are 24 people on the committee. How much will she spend on croissants? Conversation: Teacher: walk me through your solution Student: I started by calculating how many sandwiches Margaret needs to make. Since there are 24 people on the committee and each person needs 2 sandwiches, I multiplied 24 x 2 to get 48. Then I realized that each sandwich needs 2 mini croissants, so I multiplied 48 x 2 to get 96. Since the bakery offers 12 mini croissants for $8.00, I divided 96 by 12 to get 8 sets of 12 mini croissants. Finally, I multiplied 8 x $8.00 to get my final answer of $64.00.
