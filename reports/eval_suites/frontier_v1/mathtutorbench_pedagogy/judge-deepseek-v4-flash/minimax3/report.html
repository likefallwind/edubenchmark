A friend of his owns different types of cycles, which have three times are many tires as Ignatius's bikes have. He has one unicycle, a tricycle, and the rest are bikes. How many bicycles does the friend own? Conversation: Teacher: Tell me your solution Student: I think the friend has 4 bicycles. Since Ignatius owns 4 bicycles, and his friend has more tires, then his friend must have 3 x 4 = 12 bicycle tires. The unicycle has 1 tire, and the tricycle has 3 tires, so the total number of tires accounted for is 1 + 3 = 4. Therefore, the friend must have a total of 12 - 4 = 8 bicycle tires from the remaining cycles. Since each bike has 2 tires, then the friend must have 8/2 = 4 bicycles.
