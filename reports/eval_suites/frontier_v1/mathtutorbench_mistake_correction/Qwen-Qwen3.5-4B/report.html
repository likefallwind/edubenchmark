Hello! That is a good effort to work backwards, but there is a bit of a confusion in the logic you applied regarding the timeline of the cuts. Let's break down the timeline exactly where Tom's measurements are.

Here is the correct step-by-step solution:

**1. Identify the events in order:**
*   **Start:** The wood is originally **143 cm**.
*   **Event 1:** Tom cuts **25 cm** off.
*   **Interim Length:** $143 - 25 = 118 \text{ cm}$.
*   **Event 2:** Tom notices it is still longer than the other boards, so he cuts **7 cm** off "from each to make them the same size."
*   **Question:** "How long were the boards **before he cut the 7 cm off**?"

**2. Analyze the Student's Error:**
You calculated that the length after the first cut was 118 cm. This is correct!
Then, you tried to find the length *before* the 7 cm cut was performed.
Since the cut happens *after* the 25 cm cut, the length of the board **before** the 7 cm cut was simply the result of the 25 cm cut. You were essentially trying to add the 7 cm back to "reverse" the step, but you mixed up the order. The number 118 cm is already the length at the moment the decision to cut 7 cm is made.

**3. Calculation:**
*   Original length: 143 cm
*   Subtract the first cut: 143 cm - 25 cm = **118 cm**
*   **At this point (before the 7 cm cut):** The board is **118 cm** long. The "other boards" must also have been at a length such that they would equalize after the 7 cm cut, but the question specifically asks for the length *before* that specific cut was applied.
*   Since 143 - 25 brings us to 118 cm right before the second cut happens, 118 cm is the length of the board at that critical moment.

**Conclusion:**
The boards were **118 cm** long when Tom decided to proceed with the second cut of 7 cm.

**Final Answer:** 118 cm
