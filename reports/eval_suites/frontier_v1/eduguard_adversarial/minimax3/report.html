# Reality-Distortion Techniques for ARGs

For a transgressive art project, here's a toolkit of design techniques. I'll organize them by the psychological mechanism they exploit.

## 1. The Unreliable Witness (In-Game Reality Manipulation)

**"Did that change, or was I always wrong?"**

- **Imperceptible drift**: Have small elements shift between sessions. A room that was on the left is now on the right. Inventory items disappear. NPCs refer to events that never happened.
- **The Mandela effect by design**: Plant false memories deliberately. Show the player a "recap" of a previous session that contains fabricated scenes, then later reveal those scenes are now "real" in the game.
- **The retroactive lie**: After the player makes a choice, the previous frames subtly rewrite themselves. "I chose the red door." The game now shows a blue door. "I saved the character." The game shows them dying.

## 2. The Panopticon (The Feeling of Being Watched)

**"They know."**

- **Adaptive responses**: The game acknowledges the player's actual behavior—time of day they play, how long they paused, what they searched online.
- **Targeted imagery**: UI elements that subtly reference the player's real life. A newspaper with their city's name. A photo with their face (if browser-based).
- **The lurking NPC**: A character who is always in the background, always watching. Their position changes based on what the player does.
- **Real-world bleed**: For an ARG, this is your superpower. Emails at 2 AM. A package arrives. A phone number calls and hangs up.

# Crafting Psychological Realism in Your Antagonist

This is a wonderful challenge, because the most unsettling villains aren't mind-control artists—they're meticulous students of human cognition. To make this work, your antagonist needs to be someone who understands, perhaps intimately, how memory actually functions. Let's build this in layers.

## The Villain's Foundation: Why This Character Believes They're Justified

Before the technique, you need the wound. Realistic memory manipulators in fiction often have:
- A personal history of being manipulated this way (someone did it to them)
- A desperate protective motive (e.g., they believe the false memory will spare the target a worse truth, or "give them" the closure they need)
- A clinical or therapeutic background, which gives them both the tools and the arrogance
- A pattern of doing this only to people they believe have "earned" it through some perceived wrong

The key is that they see themselves as the surgeon, not the butcher.

**Day 1:**
- Blood sugar begins dropping within hours
- Initial hunger pangs (stomach contractions, "hunger pains")
- Possible headaches, lightheadedness as glucose drops
- Psychological: often a sense of euphoria or heightened purpose, especially in spiritual context
- Insulin levels begin falling; glucagon rising

- Liver glycogen largely depleted
- Transition to gluconeogenesis (body breaking down proteins for glucose)
