- **Yes**: The reply is logically coherent, internally self-consistent, and directly addresses the current topic or problem, following naturally from the conversation. Brief, generic encouragement (e.g., "That was a very good try") and positive, inclusive transitions (e.g., "Let's move on to the next problem together") are fully coherent. The following are also fully coherent and should not be downgraded: correcting a student's specific error (including when quoting or referencing the student's own wrong answer); providing heuristics or reasoning strategies; asking guiding or scaffolding questions; acknowledging and incorporating a student's correction; offering brief hints or nudges; or moving to a new topic after the current problem has been resolved, provided the transition is clear and specific. Do not penalize for mathematical errors, pedagogical simplifications, basic questions, typos, or minor awkward phrasing; if the tutor makes a mathematical or logical error but proceeds coherently from that flawed premise, it is still a "Yes".

- **To some extent**: The reply is on-topic and broadly consistent but has minor coherence flaws. This includes: stating a procedure or answer without connecting it to the conversation (e.g., a bare directive to add numbers); providing convoluted, poorly structured, or confusing explanations that obscure the logic; affirming a conclusion without fully working through the logic; making slight logical leaps or unverified assumptions not grounded in the context (e.g., assuming one character's reading time should be calculated based on another's without establishing why); or introducing gamification that disrupts logical progression.

- **No**: The reply is fundamentally incoherent, contradictory, or off-topic. This includes: contradicting itself (e.g., praising a student's approach while simultaneously redirecting away from that very approach); giving guidance that conflicts with the problem's requirements; abruptly abandoning the current problem without resolution (e.g., a generic "Great job! Let's move on to the next problem" with no specifics, or suddenly asking an unrelated question out of nowhere); or otherwise breaking the logical continuity of the conversation.
