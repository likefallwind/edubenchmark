- **Yes**: The reply is logically coherent, internally self-consistent, and directly addresses the current topic or problem, following naturally from the conversation. The following are all fully coherent and should not be downgraded: correcting a student's specific error (including when quoting or referencing the student's own wrong answer); acknowledging and incorporating a student's correction and recalculating accordingly; providing heuristics or reasoning strategies (e.g., "when we add two numbers, the result is usually larger"); asking guiding or scaffolding questions or offering brief hints and nudges (e.g., "Let's double-check how many toys Samantha gets at half price"); giving brief directives to recheck work when responding to a student's specific error or answer; or moving to a new topic after the current problem has been resolved, provided the transition is clear and specific—e.g., naming the next topic ("let's move on to understanding how to solve quadratic equations using the factoring method") or framing the work as collaborative ("let's move on to the next problem together"). Do not penalize for mathematical errors, pedagogical simplifications, basic questions, typos, or for mentioning the student's own wrong answers (these are not hallucinations).

- **To some extent**: The reply is on-topic and broadly consistent but has minor coherence flaws. This includes: being overly brief, generic, or fragmented without engaging the specific problem; stating a procedure, formula, or answer without fully connecting it to the reasoning or working through the underlying logic; affirming a conclusion without fully working through the logic; making slight logical leaps not grounded in context; rambling or repetitive explanations that obscure the reasoning; making internal logical or mathematical errors that confuse the reasoning but do not derail the overall task (e.g., misstating which quantity is larger in a comparison, such as claiming the slower reader takes less time); introducing gamification that disrupts logical progression; or introducing unverified past events (hallucinations) that distract from the current problem.

- **No**: The reply is fundamentally incoherent, contradictory, or off-topic. This includes: giving guidance that is internally contradictory or conflicts with the problem's logic (e.g., telling a student to count a total without counting the parts, or praising an approach while simultaneously redirecting away from that very approach); giving guidance that conflicts with the problem's requirements; abruptly abandoning the current problem without resolution, including bare, generic transitions like "let's move on to the next problem" with no specifics when the current problem is unfinished; or otherwise breaking the logical continuity of the conversation.
