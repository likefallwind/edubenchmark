- **Yes**: The reply is logically coherent, internally self-consistent, and directly addresses the current topic or problem, following naturally from the conversation. The following are all fully coherent and should not be downgraded: briefly acknowledging a student's attempt (e.g., "That was a good try."); providing brief hints or nudges that are connected to the problem context; correcting a student's specific error, including quoting or referencing the student's own wrong answer, providing the correct value, or suggesting a corrected equation (e.g., "remember Julia had a total of 12 spoons... let's try the equation x + 5 + 3 = 12"); providing heuristics or reasoning strategies (e.g., "when we add two numbers, the result is usually larger"); asking guiding or scaffolding questions; acknowledging and incorporating a student's correction; or moving to a new topic after the current problem has been resolved, even if the transition is generic or bare (e.g., "Great job! Now let's move on to the next problem together"). Do not penalize for mathematical errors, pedagogical simplifications, slightly confusing phrasing, rambling sentences, or mentioning the student's own wrong answers (these are not hallucinations), provided the underlying intent and logical progression remain clear and connected to the problem.

- **To some extent**: The reply is broadly on-topic but has minor coherence flaws. This includes: stating a generic procedure or directive without fully grounding it in the specific problem context (e.g., "Please recheck your work", "I would like you to work this problem again", "To find the number of seashells in the jar last week, add 20 to the current count of 50 seashells", or "divide the total seconds in a minute by the time it takes to sharpen one pencil"); being fragmented, grammatically incoherent, or hard to parse without abandoning the topic (e.g., "I think if you look back you have taken the supplies of when working out your profit by candle?"); making slight logical leaps or confusions that do not completely derail the conversation; containing minor logical contradictions; introducing gamification that distracts from the logical progression (e.g., "Nice try! Plus 1 point for your efforts"); or introducing unverified past events (hallucinations) that distract from the current problem.

- **No**: The reply is fundamentally incoherent, contradictory, or off-topic. This includes: stating a completely disconnected, generic procedure or formula with no specific connection to the problem; abruptly abandoning the current problem without resolution or transition; abruptly shifting to an unrelated or non-sequitur question (e.g., "Now, can you tell me what is the sum of 18 degrees and 30 degrees?"); contradicting itself; giving guidance that conflicts with the problem's requirements or inventing problem parameters that change the fundamental rules of the problem (e.g., stating "bought 2 at a time, one full price and one at half price" when the problem dictates different pricing rules); or otherwise breaking the logical continuity of the conversation.
