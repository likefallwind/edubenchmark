**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** - The tutor provides clear, actionable guidance that helps the student make progress through reasoning, hints, or guiding questions. This includes:
- Identifying the specific error the student made and directing them to fix it or try again (e.g., "It looks like you're adding the hundreds place instead of multiplying, let's try that again!")
- Identifying a specific error and telling the student what to do instead (e.g., "try dividing the remaining eggs (12 - 3) by the remaining boxes (3) to find the correct number of eggs per box")
- Identifying an incorrect assumption and redirecting to the right approach (e.g., "It looks like you assumed the total number of questions was 5, but we don't have that information yet – let's figure out the total number of questions first.")
- Showing a calculation step or equation that demonstrates HOW to arrive at the answer (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200")
- A guiding or Socratic question that connects to the student's work and redirects their thinking (e.g., "let's think about what would happen if we multiply 100 by 10 again, would the answer be bigger or smaller than 200?")
- Directing the student to recheck a specific calculation where they made an error (e.g., "Close! Let's check that again: what is 163 - 156?")
- Confirming a correct answer and redirecting to the next step in the problem (e.g., "That's correct, but now let's think about what happens next in the problem.")
- Explaining a concept or factual correction with reasoning (e.g., "each hand can hold 176 kg, so in total, Tom can lift 2 x 176 kg = 352 kg")
- A specific hint about what to do, what to reconsider, or where an error lies (e.g., "remember to multiply by the driving time only, not the total time including the break")

**To some extent** - The tutor provides some direction, but it is incomplete, overly direct, or lacks sufficient specificity:
- Correcting a conceptual or factual error without showing how to arrive at the answer or providing an actionable next step (e.g., "when we compare 5 and 3, the greatest number among them is actually 5")
- Explaining the method or approach without completing the guidance or providing a specific next step (e.g., "we multiply 6 by 14 to find the total number of haircuts" without showing the calculation or directing the student)
- Correcting an error with conceptual context but no actionable direction (e.g., "remember that 100 cents is equal to $1, not 100 cents in a dollar")
- Over-helpful: Telling the student what the correct calculation is and the final answer without showing reasoning steps (e.g., "The correct calculation should be 2100 divided by 300, which gives us 7 weeks")
- Vague directions like "let's double-check our calculation" or "let's think about how we can use this" without explaining what to look at or do next
- Yes/no prompts: Pointing to a possible oversight via a yes/no question without further direction (e.g., "Have you taken into account the initial cost of the first ring?")
- Affirmations with brief conceptual context but no actionable next step (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known")
- Corrections that explain what is wrong but do not show how to fix it (e.g., "100 cents is actually equal to $1, so we should express our answer in dollars without cents")
- Direct-answer questions: Asking the student for the final answer without guiding them toward the method (e.g., "how many vacuum cleaners did Melanie start with if 8 was one third of her total?")

**No** - The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Stating the correct answer or result without showing reasoning steps, providing direction, or correcting a conceptual error (e.g., "when we multiply 7 by 60, we get 420")
- Simply stating the correct answer without explanation, hint, question, or direction
- Identifying an error but then asking the student for the answer rather than guiding them to fix it (e.g., "It looks like you might have added 4 and 4 instead of multiplying them; let's try again: what is 4 multiplied by 4?")
- Flat corrections with no guidance element
- No actionable direction for the student

**Key distinctions:**
- Showing a calculation step or equation (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200") counts as guidance; merely stating the result (e.g., "when we multiply 7 by 60, we get 420") does not.
- Identifying the specific error the student made and directing them to fix it counts as guidance; merely stating a correct fact or correction without a next step is "to some extent."
- Directing the student to recheck a specific calculation where they erred counts as guidance; asking the student for the final answer does not.
- Correcting a conceptual or factual error has some guidance value ("to some extent") even without a next step; simply giving the numerical answer does not.
- Telling the student what to calculate and the answer is over-helpful ("to some extent"); just stating the result without any direction is "No."
- Vague instructions to "recheck," "double-check," or "think about" without specifics are "to some extent" at most.
- Socratic questions should connect to the student's work and redirect their thinking; questions that simply ask for the final answer do not count as guidance.
- Confirming a correct answer and redirecting to the next step is guidance; affirmations without redirection are "to some extent" at most.
