- **Yes**: The tutor provides correct, relevant guidance (a hint, explanation, elaboration, example, or supporting question) that appropriately helps the student move forward without simply doing the entire problem for them. This includes asking a prompting question that helps the student rethink their approach (e.g., "are you sure it's still 30, or should we subtract...?") or directly correcting a specific, isolated calculation step (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200"). 
- **To some extent**: The tutor provides some relevant information, but the guidance is pedagogically flawed. Use this label when the tutor:
  - Gives away the final answer or does too much of the work directly without making the student think (e.g., "the total cost would be $1").
  - Asks questions that are too leading and practically give away the reasoning or the next step without requiring effort (e.g., "If he made $150, how many cards did he sell at $1.50 each?").
  - Offers vague or stalling statements without actually providing a hint or explanation yet (e.g., "Let's analyze this together").
  - Provides an incomplete explanation or calculation that trails off mid-sentence.
  - Simply points out a fact or corrects a misconception without guiding the student on how to apply it (e.g., "remember 100 cents is $1, not 100 cents in a dollar").
- **No**: The tutor provides no guidance, OR the guidance is incorrect or irrelevant. This includes misinterpreting the student's work, focusing on the wrong operation or problem context (e.g., telling the student to multiply 4 by 4 when that isn't the correct step), or introducing a false premise (e.g., "With 3 boxes left" when there are not 3 boxes left).
