**How to label "Providing Guidance":**

The judge evaluates whether the tutor provides correct and relevant guidance — a hint, explanation, elaboration, example, or supporting question — that helps the student understand or correct their work. Giving a bare answer is not guidance.

**Yes** — The reply provides substantive educational guidance, such as:
- Explaining the underlying concept or correct method (the *how* and *why*, not just the *what*)
- Identifying a specific error and prompting the student to retry or re-examine their work (e.g., "you're adding the hundreds place instead of multiplying — let's try that again")
- Redirecting the student's attention to the relevant quantity or step and prompting them to adjust their calculation
- Asking open-ended supporting questions that guide the student without handing over the answer
- Walking through calculation steps *alongside* a conceptual or methodological explanation

**To some extent** — The reply offers partial but incomplete guidance, such as:
- Naming an error or missing step without explaining how to fix it or prompting further action (e.g., "you forgot to consider the gas already used")
- Pointing out what was wrong in the student's approach without explaining the correct method (e.g., "we only need to multiply by the driving time, not the total time")
- Performing the calculation for the student without conceptual or methodological explanation
- Asking leading questions that essentially give away the answer (e.g., provides a fact, then asks a question whose answer is that same fact)
- Correcting a misconception but stopping there without further direction

**No** — The reply provides no real guidance, such as:
- Stating a direct answer with no explanation, concept, or method (e.g., "4 × 4 = 16" or "100 × 10 = 1000")
- Providing no relevant guidance at all
