**Yes** — The tutor provides effective, correct, and relevant guidance that helps the student move forward:
- Identifies the specific error or area of difficulty and prompts the student to retry or recheck without giving the answer (e.g., "It looks like you're adding the hundreds place instead of multiplying, let's try that again!")
- Points to where a specific error is and suggests collaborative rechecking (e.g., "It seems there might be a small mistake in your calculation for the total from the two other harbors; let's recheck that part together.")
- Confirms correct work and provides a clear, complete explanation of the reasoning (e.g., "You are correct that it's 7 sets of three days or 21 days, but the more accurate explanation is that 3 days make up a week, so 21 days is equivalent to 21/3 = 7 weeks.")

**To some extent** — The tutor provides some correct and relevant guidance but it is limited:
- Too minimal or vague: redirects to revisit a concept or area without identifying the specific error or giving a concrete hint (e.g., "let's revisit the number of free haircuts Tammy has actually received so far, as that's a key part of solving this problem accurately.")
- Gives away too much: shows calculations or walks through steps to the final answer, even if accompanied by a reminder or prompt — the student receives the answer rather than being guided to find it (e.g., "remember that Tammy has only received 5 free haircuts so far, so she has actually had 5 * 14 = 70 haircuts. Then, when you add the 5 haircuts she is away from another free one, it totals to 75 haircuts.")
- Affirms an observation but with only slight elaboration (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known.")
- Asks a supporting question that is too direct, simple, or lacks sufficient context (e.g., "If he made $150, how many cards did he sell at $1.50 each?")

**No** — The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Directly states the correct answer or correction without any element that engages the student's thinking — no meaningful hint, reminder of a key fact, question, or prompt to retry. A brief filler phrase like "let's double-check" immediately followed by stating the answer does not count as guidance. (e.g., "one and a half increase in productivity actually means the productivity is 150% of the original, not 150% more, so the Albaszu machine is now cutting 10 x 1.5 = 15 trees daily, not 10 + 10 x 1.5 = 25 trees.")
- Provides no guidance at all
- Provides incorrect or irrelevant guidance
