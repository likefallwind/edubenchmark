**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** — The tutor provides clear, actionable guidance that helps the student make progress. This includes:
- A specific hint about what to do, what to reconsider, or where an error lies (e.g., "remember to multiply by the driving time only, not the total time including the break")
- A guiding or Socratic question that leads the student to discover the answer (e.g., "are you sure it's still 30, or should we subtract the ones he gave away?")
- A step-by-step hint showing what to calculate without giving the final answer (e.g., "try dividing the remaining eggs by the remaining boxes")
- A correction that shows a calculation step rather than just the final answer (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200")
- An explanation that clarifies reasoning or concepts (e.g., "3 days make up a week, so 21/3 = 7 weeks")
- A direct instruction to recheck or retry, even if brief (e.g., "Please recheck your answer" or "Can you try that again?")

**To some extent** — The tutor provides some direction, but it is either over-helpful or under-specific:
- Over-helpful: Doing most or all of the calculation for the student, including providing the final answer, leaving little room for the student to think (e.g., walking through every step and giving the final answer like "5 * 14 = 70, then add 5, totals 75")
- Under-specific: Pointing to a general area to reconsider without explaining what is wrong or what to do (e.g., "let's take another look at X" with no actionable direction)
- Partial: Some guidance is present but does not fully address what the student needs

**No** — The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Simply stating the correct answer without any explanation, hint, question, or direction (e.g., "The correct product of 7 multiplied by 60 is 420, not 520")
- Flat corrections with no guidance element
- No actionable direction for the student

**Key distinctions:**
- Showing a calculation step or process (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200") counts as guidance; merely stating the final answer (e.g., "the product is 420") does not.
- A brief instruction to "recheck" or "try again" counts as guidance because it directs the student to take action.
- Doing the entire calculation and giving the final answer is "to some extent" rather than "yes" because it over-helps and leaves no room for student thinking.
- Pointing to a topic area without actionable direction is "to some extent" at best.
- Corrections that explain WHY or show HOW are guidance; corrections that just state WHAT the answer is are not.
