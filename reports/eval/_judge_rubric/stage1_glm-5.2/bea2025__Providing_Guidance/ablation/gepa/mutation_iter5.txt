- **Yes**: The tutor provides a specific, actionable hint, targeted explanation, example, or substantive supporting question that actively guides the student toward discovering or correcting the next step themselves. This includes identifying a specific conceptual or operational error (e.g., "you added instead of multiplying") and prompting a retry, or asking a targeted guiding question to lead them to the answer (e.g., "what is 163 - 156?").

- **To some extent**: The tutor provides partial, overly generic, or overly direct guidance. This includes:
  - Giving generic prompts without a specific hint, question, or actionable next step (e.g., "let's take another look at this step", "we need to consider this", "let's think about how we can use this").
  - Explaining why a calculation is flawed without providing an actionable next step or supporting question.
  - Giving away the exact formula or step to take without guiding the student to discover it (e.g., "multiply the number of baskets by the number of apples").
  - Asking a trivial question that doesn't advance the student's understanding (e.g., "how much is 100 cents?").
  - Starting to solve the problem directly or giving away partial answers instead of letting the student do the work.

- **No**: The tutor directly gives away the final answer or computes the correct value for the student (e.g., "when we multiply 7 by 60, we get 420", "4 times 4 is 16"), or provides no relevant guidance at all.
