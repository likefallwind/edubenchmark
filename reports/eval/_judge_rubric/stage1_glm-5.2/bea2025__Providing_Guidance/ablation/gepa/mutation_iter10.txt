**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** - The tutor provides clear, actionable guidance that helps the student make progress through reasoning, hints, or guiding questions. This includes:
- Identifying the specific error the student made and directing them to fix it or try again (e.g., "It looks like you're adding the hundreds place instead of multiplying, let's try that again!")
- Identifying a specific error and telling the student what to do instead (e.g., "try dividing the remaining eggs (12 - 3) by the remaining boxes (3) to find the correct number of eggs per box")
- Identifying an incorrect assumption and redirecting to the right approach (e.g., "It looks like you assumed the total number of questions was 5, but we don't have that information yet – let's figure out the total number of questions first.")
- Directing the student to recheck or show their work on a specific calculation, value, or step (e.g., "Let's double-check that subtraction together – can you show me how you got 13?")
- Pointing the student to a specific element of their own work to re-examine (e.g., "let's take a closer look at your equation 2(x + 0.25) = 45... there might be a small mistake in how we're representing the cost")
- A guiding or Socratic question that connects to the student's work and redirects their thinking, including yes/no questions that point to a specific oversight (e.g., "Have you used those 2000 steps anywhere in your solution?")
- Directing the student to recheck a specific calculation where they made an error (e.g., "Close! Let's check that again: what is 163 - 156?")
- Confirming a correct answer and redirecting to the next step in the problem (e.g., "That's correct, but now let's think about what happens next in the problem.")
- Reminding the student of the correct concept or approach and directing them to retry (e.g., "remember we're looking for the greatest number between 5 and 3. Let's try again.")
- Explaining a concept or factual correction with reasoning AND an actionable next step or direction for what to do (e.g., "each hand can hold 176 kg, so in total Tom can lift 2 × 176 = 352 kg – now try calculating the total for all three boxes")
- A specific hint about what to do, what to reconsider, or where an error lies (e.g., "remember to multiply by the driving time only, not the total time including the break")

**To some extent** - The tutor provides some direction, but it is incomplete, overly direct, lacks specificity, or gives away too much of the answer:
- Asking the student to retry a calculation with only a generic hint, without identifying the specific error (e.g., "Let's try multiplying 7 and 60 again, remember to carry over any extra tens!")
- Showing a calculation step that gives an intermediate or partial answer along with some direction for next steps, but is partially over-helpful (e.g., "2 rides × 33 dollars/ride would actually be 66 dollars, but we have already accounted for the 20.25 and 2.25 in our initial calculations, so we need to add the remaining balance...")
- Explaining the method or approach without completing the guidance or providing a specific next step
- Over-helpful: Telling the student what the correct calculation is and the final answer without showing reasoning steps (e.g., "The correct calculation should be 2100 divided by 300, which gives us 7 weeks")
- Vague directions like "let's double-check our calculation" or "let's think about how we can use this" without explaining what to look at or do next
- Affirmations with brief conceptual context but no actionable next step (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known")
- Corrections that explain what is wrong but do not show how to fix it or direct the student onward

**No** - The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Simply stating the correct answer or result, with or without showing the calculation, but without providing direction, a next step, or identifying what the student should do (e.g., "Actually, the correct product of 7 multiplied by 60 is 420, not 520.")
- Stating the correct answer with calculation/reasoning but no actionable direction or next step (e.g., "the Albaszu machine is now cutting 10 × 1.5 = 15 trees daily, not 10 + 10 × 1.5 = 25 trees.")
- Asking the student to compute the next step's answer or final answer without guiding them toward the method (e.g., "Great job calculating the weight per box; now, can you use that to find the total weight of all three remaining boxes?")
- Simply stating the correct answer without explanation, hint, question, or direction
- Identifying an error but then asking the student for the answer rather than guiding them to fix it (e.g., "It looks like you might have added 4 and 4 instead of multiplying them; what is 4 multiplied by 4?")
- Flat corrections with no guidance element or direction for what to do next
- No actionable direction for the student

**Key distinctions:**
- Asking "can you show me how you got X?" about a specific calculation counts as guidance (Yes); asking "what is X?" or "can you find X?" to elicit a final or next-step answer does not.
- Directing attention to a specific element of the student's own work (a specific equation, value, or step) counts as guidance (Yes); vague "let's look at this" without specifics is "To some extent" at most.
- Yes/no questions that point to a specific oversight count as guidance (Yes); vague yes/no prompts without pointing to a specific issue are "To some extent" at most.
- Identifying a specific error and directing the student to fix it counts as guidance (Yes); merely stating a correct fact or correction without a next step is "No."
- Reminding the student of the correct concept and directing them to retry counts as guidance (Yes); generic hints like "remember to carry over" without identifying the specific error are "To some extent."
- Showing a calculation step that gives the final answer with no further direction is "No"; showing a calculation step that gives an intermediate answer while directing the student onward can be "To some extent" or "Yes" depending on how much direction is given.
- Asking the student to compute the next step's answer without guiding the method is "No"; guiding them through the method with a Socratic question is "Yes."
- Correcting a conceptual or factual error without any actionable next step or direction is "No" (not "To some extent").
- Vague instructions to "recheck," "double-check," or "think about" without specifics are "To some extent" at most; but directing the student to recheck a specific calculation or value counts as guidance (Yes).
- Socratic questions should connect to the student's work and redirect their thinking; questions that simply ask for the final answer do not count as guidance.
- Confirming a correct answer and redirecting to the next step is guidance (Yes); affirmations without redirection are "To some extent" at most.
