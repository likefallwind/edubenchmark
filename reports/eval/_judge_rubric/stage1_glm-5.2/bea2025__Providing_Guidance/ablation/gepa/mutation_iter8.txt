**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** - The tutor provides clear, actionable guidance that helps the student make progress through reasoning, hints, or guiding questions. This includes:
- A specific hint about what to do, what to reconsider, or where an error lies (e.g., "remember to multiply by the driving time only, not the total time including the break")
- A guiding or Socratic question that redirects the student's thinking toward the right approach or next step (e.g., "let's think about what the problem is asking us to calculate - her annual pension after 30 years")
- Showing a reasoning process or calculation step that helps the student understand HOW to arrive at the answer (e.g., "7 times 60 is the same as 7 times 6 tens, which is 42 tens or 420" or "since 10 times 10 is 100, we need to multiply 100 by 10, so it would be 1000")
- Explaining a concept or factual correction with reasoning (e.g., "each hand can hold 176 kg, so in total, Tom can lift 2 x 176 kg = 352 kg")
- An explanation that clarifies reasoning or concepts (e.g., "3 days make up a week, so 21/3 = 7 weeks")

**To some extent** - The tutor provides some direction, but it is incomplete, overly direct, or lacks sufficient specificity:
- Over-helpful: Stating the correct calculation and final answer directly without showing reasoning steps (e.g., "The correct calculation should be 2100 divided by 300, which gives us 7 weeks")
- Under-specific: Vague directions like "let's double-check our calculation" or "let's think about how we can use this" without explaining what to look at or do next
- Direct-answer questions: Asking the student for the final answer without guiding them toward the method (e.g., "how many vacuum cleaners did Melanie start with if 8 was one third of her total?")
- Yes/no prompts: Pointing to a possible oversight via a yes/no question without further direction (e.g., "Have you taken into account the initial cost of the first ring?")
- Affirmations with brief conceptual context but no actionable next step (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known")
- Corrections that explain what is wrong but do not show how to fix it (e.g., "100 cents is actually equal to $1, so we should express our answer in dollars without cents")

**No** - The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Simply stating the correct answer without explanation, hint, question, or direction
- Flat corrections with no guidance element
- Asking the student to perform a trivial arithmetic calculation without connecting it to reasoning or problem-solving (e.g., "what is 4 multiplied by 4?")
- No actionable direction for the student

**Key distinctions:**
- Showing a reasoning process or calculation steps that explain HOW counts as guidance; merely restating the calculation and result does not.
- Vague instructions to "recheck," "double-check," or "think about" without specifics are "to some extent" at most, not "yes."
- Socratic questions should redirect the student's thinking toward the right approach; questions that simply ask for the final answer or a basic arithmetic fact do not count as full guidance.
- Corrections that explain WHY or show HOW with reasoning are guidance; corrections that just state the answer or what is wrong are not.
- Affirmations that confirm the student's approach, even with brief explanation, are "to some extent" if they do not provide an actionable next step.
