**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** - The tutor provides clear, actionable guidance that helps the student make progress through reasoning, hints, or guiding questions. This includes:
- Identifying the specific error the student made and directing them to fix it or try again (e.g., "It looks like you're adding the hundreds place instead of multiplying, let's try that again!")
- Identifying a specific error and telling the student what to do instead (e.g., "try dividing the remaining eggs (12 - 3) by the remaining boxes (3) to find the correct number of eggs per box")
- Identifying an incorrect assumption and redirecting to the right approach (e.g., "It looks like you assumed the total number of questions was 5, but we don't have that information yet – let's figure out the total number of questions first.")
- Showing a calculation step or equation that demonstrates HOW to arrive at the answer (e.g., "100 * 2 = 200")
- A guiding or Socratic question that connects to the student's work and redirects their thinking (e.g., "let's think about what would happen if we multiply 100 by 10 again, would the answer be bigger or smaller than 200?")
- Directing the student to recheck or recalculate a specific calculation where they made an error (e.g., "let's recalculate 7 x 60 together to make sure we get the correct answer", "Close! Let's check that again: what is 163 - 156?")
- Confirming a correct answer and redirecting to the next step in the problem (e.g., "That's correct, but now let's think about what happens next in the problem.")
- Explaining a concept or factual correction with reasoning (e.g., "each hand can hold 176 kg, so in total, Tom can lift 2 x 176 kg = 352 kg")
- A specific hint about what to do, what to reconsider, or where an error lies (e.g., "remember to multiply by the driving time only, not the total time including the break")
- Telling the student to recheck their answer when framed as a clear, actionable next step (e.g., "Please recheck your answer.")

**To some extent** - The tutor provides some direction, but it is incomplete, overly direct, lacks sufficient specificity, or asks for the final answer:
- Correcting a conceptual or factual error without showing how to arrive at the answer or providing a specific actionable next step (e.g., "Madeline's monthly expense on food is $100, not her yearly expense on medicine", "the 8 gallons is the amount she used, not what's left in the tank", "100 cents is equal to $1, so the total cost would be $1")
- Asking a simplified or basic guiding question to check conceptual understanding (e.g., "which number is greater - 5 or 3?")
- Explaining the method or approach without completing the guidance or providing a specific next step (e.g., "we multiply 6 by 14 to find the total number of haircuts" without showing the calculation or directing the student)
- Vague directions like "let's double-check our calculation" or "let's think about how we can use this" without explaining what to look at or do next
- Yes/no prompts: Pointing to a possible oversight via a yes/no question without further direction (e.g., "does the equation 3x/2 indeed equal 150 when x equals 100?")
- Affirmations with brief conceptual context but no actionable next step (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known")
- Direct-answer questions: Asking the student for the final answer without guiding them toward the method (e.g., "If he made $150, how many cards did he sell at $1.50 each?")

**No** - The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Stating the correct answer or result of a calculation without showing reasoning steps, providing direction, or correcting a conceptual error (e.g., "when we multiply 7 by 60, we get 420", "Actually, the correct product of 7 multiplied by 60 is 420, not 520.")
- Providing the full correct calculation and final answer, solving the problem entirely for the student without leaving any steps for them to do (e.g., "the Albaszu machine is now cutting 10 x 1.5 = 15 trees daily, not 10 + 10 x 1.5 = 25 trees.")
- Flat corrections of a calculation result with no guidance element (e.g., "when we multiply 4 by 4, the correct answer is actually 16.")
- Generic commands to recheck with zero specifics (e.g., "Kindly recheck your answer.")
- Simply stating the correct answer without explanation, hint, question, or direction
- No actionable direction for the student

**Key distinctions:**
- Correcting a conceptual or factual error (e.g., mixing up values, misinterpreting what a number represents) has some guidance value ("to some extent") even if the final answer is stated; however, merely stating the result of an arithmetic calculation (e.g., "7 * 60 = 420") without any conceptual correction is "No".
- Providing the full correct calculation and final answer to a multi-step problem is "No" because it does all the work for the student.
- Directing the student to recheck a *specific* calculation where they erred (e.g., "let's recalculate 7 x 60 together") counts as guidance ("Yes"); asking them to "recheck" with no specifics is "To some extent" at best, and generic commands like "Kindly recheck your answer" are "No".
- Socratic questions should connect to the student's work and redirect their thinking; questions that simply ask for the final answer are "To some extent".
- Yes/no prompts that give away the answer or point to an oversight without further direction are "To some extent".
