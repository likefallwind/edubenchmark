**Scoring Guidelines for "Providing Guidance"**

**Yes** - The tutor provides meaningful guidance that helps the student make progress. This includes:
- Pointing to a specific step, calculation, or aspect of the problem to examine or reconsider (e.g., "let's take a closer look at how we're calculating the yearly food expense")
- Identifying and explaining a conceptual misunderstanding without simply giving the correct answer (e.g., "'one and a half increase' refers to 150% of the original value being added, not 150% of the original value itself")
- Introducing a key insight or consideration the student missed, even if a calculation follows (e.g., "Jim is also out of pocket for half the cost of his own ring, so the total is $25,000")
- Directing the student to the next step or what to think about next (e.g., "let's think about what happens next in the problem")
- Offering to work through something step by step with the student
- A guiding question that prompts thinking without revealing the full approach

**To some extent** - The tutor provides some direction or relevant information, but it is over-helpful, under-specific, or incomplete:
- Over-helpful: Revealing the full approach, calculation, or final answer (e.g., "dividing the two together would provide the answer in weeks, don't you think?" or "the correct calculation is 2100/300 = 7 weeks")
- Telling the student exactly what to do without explaining why or inviting them to think (e.g., "you need to subtract $4.50 from the ticket price, not the other way around")
- Stating the correct answer or value with brief context but without deeper explanation (e.g., "the total cost for all three tickets is $20.25, not per ticket")
- Explaining the correct interpretation and giving the specific value directly, leaving little for the student to figure out (e.g., "the 4 gallons brought the tank to its full capacity of 12 gallons")
- Generic instruction on how to solve (e.g., "multiply the number of baskets by the number of apples in each basket")
- Affirming a correct approach without further direction or elaboration (e.g., "Multiplication is indeed used to find the total distance when speed and time are known")
- Providing general formulas or methods without applying them to the specific problem (e.g., stating the quadratic formula without working through the specific problem)

**No** - The tutor provides no real guidance:
- Simply stating the correct answer with no explanation, context, or direction
- Flat corrections with no guidance element
- No actionable direction, explanation, or relevant information

**Key distinctions:**
- Pointing to a specific step or calculation to reconsider is Yes; vague pointers without specificity are To some extent.
- Explaining a conceptual misunderstanding without giving the answer is Yes; giving the correct value directly is To some extent.
- Introducing a new insight is Yes even when the answer is shown; merely correcting a calculation is To some extent.
- Telling the student exactly what to do is To some extent.
- Socratic questions that reveal the approach are To some extent (over-helpful).
- Affirming correct approaches or providing general formulas is To some extent.
- Corrections that explain WHY or introduce new insights are Yes; corrections that just state WHAT the answer is are No.
