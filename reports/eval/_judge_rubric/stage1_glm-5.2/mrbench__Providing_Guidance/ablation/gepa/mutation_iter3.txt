A tutor reply "provides guidance" when it gives the student concrete, correct, and relevant help making progress on the problem—via a hint, explanation, worked example, targeted question, or direct correction. The guidance must be specific to the problem and must give the student new information, direction, or a useful focus that helps them move forward—not just generic advice, encouragement, or a proposal to "work through it together."

**Yes** - The tutor provides specific, correct, and relevant guidance that directly helps the student move forward. The guidance gives new information, direction, or a useful focus tied to the problem. Examples:
- A direct correction or worked example that addresses the student's specific mathematical error or misconception with correct reasoning or calculation (e.g., "If Sid had $4 more than half of his original money left, he would have $24 + $4 = $28 left, not $40")
- A specific hint or procedural instruction tailored to the problem that points out exactly what to fix or how to apply a rule (e.g., "only the sprouted seeds that are eaten are lost, so half of the 150 seeds, which is 75, are still able to flower," or "you only need to subtract the bricks for the two courses that weren't built on the last wall, not for all four walls")
- A targeted question that directs the student to re-examine a specific calculation or step (e.g., "Let's double-check that calculation together - can you show me how you arrived at 6?")

**To some extent** - The tutor engages with the problem and provides some direction, but the guidance is incomplete, lacks full reasoning, or only addresses surface details. Examples:
- Pointing out a specific error or stating a relevant rule/constraint, but not fully explaining how to fix the error or apply the rule to reach the solution (e.g., "remember that Carmen only bids after the other three people, so she doesn't make every bid")
- Correcting only a surface detail such as terminology or definitions without guiding the student on the actual problem (e.g., "a 5-sided polygon is actually called a pentagon")
- General probing questions that lack a specific direction or focus (e.g., "Could you explain how you got 4?")
- Stating a fact or definition without connecting it to how to apply it
- Guidance that contains an error in reasoning or calculation (the tutor is still engaged, but the guidance is flawed)

**No** - The tutor provides no actual guidance, direction, or useful focus. Examples:
- Merely stating the answer is incorrect (e.g., "Sorry, your answer is incorrect") or vaguely pointing out a missing element without any concept, rule, or direction (e.g., "It seems you might have overlooked including the graduates themselves in the total count of attendees")
- Generic questions asking for the next step or asking to "try again" without giving any new information, hint, or specific focus (e.g., "Can you tell me what the next step would be to solve this equation?")
- Generic encouragement, restating the problem, or just proposing to work through something together without any specific hint, fact, method, or focus

Note: A direct correction, worked example, or targeted question counts as Yes when it is correct and clearly addresses the student's confusion with appropriate reasoning or focus. The grading reflects whether concrete, correct, specific guidance is provided—not whether the tutor avoids giving the answer.
