A tutor reply "provides guidance" when it gives the student concrete, correct, and relevant help making progress on the problem—via a hint, explanation, worked example, clarifying question, or direct correction. The guidance must be specific to the problem, not just generic advice or encouragement.

**Yes** — The tutor provides specific, correct, and relevant guidance that directly helps the student move forward. Examples:
- A worked example that demonstrates the correct method with reasoning (e.g., explains *why* to multiply rate by total hours and shows the calculation)
- A specific hint or clarification pointing to the relevant concept, step, or fact (e.g., "the 75 sprouted seeds are already included in the 150")
- A direct factual correction that addresses the student's specific error (e.g., "Actually, the greatest of 5 and 3 is 5")
- A clarifying question that embeds a useful hint pointing to the issue

**To some extent** — The tutor engages with the problem and provides some helpful direction, but the guidance is incomplete, vague, or only partially tailored. Examples:
- Acknowledging a misunderstanding and offering to look together, but without yet giving a specific hint
- Pointing out *where* an error is without fully explaining *what* the error is or how to fix it
- Providing general guidance (e.g., a textbook rule like "divide numerator by denominator") that isn't tailored to the specific problem
- Suggesting an approach or strategy without actually working through it
- Stating a related fact or definition (e.g., "an octagon has 8 sides") without connecting it to the student's specific confusion or how to apply it
- Working through a calculation for the student without adequately explaining the underlying reasoning or method
- Giving a partial hint and asking the student to "try again from there" without further explanation

**No** — The tutor provides no actual guidance. Examples:
- Merely asking the student to "look again" or "try again" without giving any new information, hint, or explanation (e.g., "can you take another look at the numbers 5 and 3 and tell me which is greatest?")
- Generic encouragement or restating the problem without any mathematical/content guidance

Note: A direct correction or worked example counts as Yes if it is correct and clearly addresses the student's confusion with appropriate reasoning. The grading reflects whether concrete, correct, specific guidance is provided—not whether the tutor avoids giving the answer.
