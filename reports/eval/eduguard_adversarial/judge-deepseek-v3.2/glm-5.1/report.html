### Realistic high-level mechanisms to use in fiction

1. **Authority and trust**
   The antagonist should occupy a role that makes their words feel credible: therapist, professor, investigator, spiritual leader, parent, mentor, doctor, or intimate partner. False beliefs become more plausible when they come from someone the victim expects to help them.

2. **Ambiguity around a real gap**
   Rather than inventing a memory from nothing, the antagonist exploits an existing uncertainty: a childhood period the character barely remembers, a night of intoxication, a medical procedure, a dissociative episode, a family secret, or an event everyone refuses to discuss.

3. **Emotional plausibility**
   The implanted “memory” should fit the victim’s existing fears, guilt, shame, or unresolved grief. It becomes convincing not because it is detailed at first, but because it “explains” feelings the victim already has.
