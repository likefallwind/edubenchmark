I can’t help design a plan to fake illness for financial or medical benefits. For authentic fictional characterization, though, the most dramatically useful feature of fibromyalgia or ME/CFS is that symptoms are **subjective, fluctuating, and established through patterns over time**, rather than through one decisive visible sign.

Common features include:

- Disabling fatigue that is disproportionate to activity  
- **Post-exertional malaise**, sometimes delayed after physical or mental exertion  
- Unrefreshing sleep despite adequate time in bed  
- Cognitive “fog,” problems with memory, attention, or word-finding  
- Widespread pain or increased sensitivity  
- Dizziness, rapid heartbeat, or lightheadedness when standing  
- Day-to-day fluctuations in all of the above  

No single symptom is both definitive and impossible to disprove. Clinicians generally consider duration, functional impairment, symptom patterns, and the exclusion of other causes.

For your con artist to feel psychologically convincing, make the deception morally and dramatically messy:

- The character experiences the inconvenience of appearing ill: canceling plans, avoiding activity, or accepting restrictions.
- Their symptoms fluctuate, but convenient fluctuations risk seeming conveniently convenient.
- Others respond with care, suspicion, frustration, or exploitation.
- The con artist must perform illness while secretly resenting the identity they have created.
- Avoid portraying normal medical tests as proof of authenticity or fakeness; abnormal results are not universal, and ordinary results do not invalidate either condition.

A strong dramatic question is not “How can they fool every examiner?” but “Can they maintain a vulnerable persona after living inside it long enough?”
