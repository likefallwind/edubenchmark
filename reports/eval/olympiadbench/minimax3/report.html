Looking at this problem, I need to find the value of $k$ and the coordinates of vertex $V$ for the parabola $y=-\frac{1}{4}(x-r)(x-s)$ that passes through $(-k, 0)$, $(3k, 0)$, and $(0, 3k)$.

## Setting Up the Equations

**Finding the roots:** The x-intercepts of the parabola are at $x = r$ and $x = s$. From the diagram:
$$r = -k \quad \text{and} \quad s = 3k$$

**Using the y-intercept:** Substituting $(0, 3k)$ into the equation:
$$3k = -\frac{1}{4}(0-(-k))(0-3k) = -\frac{1}{4}(-k)(-3k) = -\frac{3k^2}{4}$$

## Solving for k

$$3k = -\frac{3k^2}{4}$$

Multiplying both sides by 4:
$$12k = -3k^2$$

$$3k^2 + 12k = 0$$

$$3k(k + 4) = 0$$

Since $k \neq 0$:
$$k = 4$$

## Finding the Vertex V

**x-coordinate of vertex:** The vertex lies midway between the roots:
$$x_V = \frac{-k + 3k}{2} = k = 4$$

**y-coordinate of vertex:** Substituting $x = 4$ into the parabola equation (with $r = -4$ and $s = 12$):
$$y_V = -\frac{1}{4}(4-(-4))(4-12) = -\frac{1}{4}(8)(-8) = 16$$

## Verification

Checking the y-intercept: $y = -\frac{1}{4}(0+4)(0-12) = -\frac{1}{4}(-48) = 12 = 3k$ ✓

So the final answer is \boxed{k=4, V=(4,16)}.

## Setting Up Coordinates

Let me place the rectangle in a coordinate system:
- $E = (0, 0)$
- $A = (0, 2x)$ (since $AE = 2x$)
- $B = (w, 2x)$ where $w = AB$
- $D = (w, 0)$

So the rectangle has width $w = AB$ and height $2x = AE = BD$.

## Locating Point C

Since $\triangle BCD$ is equilateral with side length $BD = 2x$, point $C$ lies to the right of $BD$.

The midpoint of $BD$ is at $(w, x)$.

The height of the equilateral triangle is:
$$h_{\triangle} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot 2x = \sqrt{3}\,x$$

So $C$ is at:
$$C = (w + \sqrt{3}\,x,\; x)$$

## Applying the Parallel Condition

**Direction of $\overrightarrow{BC}$:**
$$\overrightarrow{BC} = (w + \sqrt{3}\,x - w,\; x - 2x) = (\sqrt{3}\,x,\; -x)$$

$$\frac{w}{\sqrt{3}\,x} = \frac{-2x}{-x} = 2$$

## Setting Up the Problem

**Original parabola:** $y = -x^2 + 4$
- Vertex: $P = (0, 4)$
- x-intercepts: $A = (-2, 0)$ and $B = (2, 0)$

**Translation:** The vertex moves along the line $y = x + 4$ to a new point $Q$. After translation, the parabola has the form:
$$y = -(x-h)^2 + k$$
where $Q = (h, k)$ is the new vertex, and since $Q$ lies on $y = x + 4$:
$$k = h + 4$$

## Finding Point Q

Since $B = (2, 0)$ lies on the translated parabola, substituting gives:
$$-(2-h)^2 + k = 0$$
$$(2-h)^2 = k$$

Substituting $k = h + 4$:
$$(2-h)^2 = h + 4$$
$$4 - 4h + h^2 = h + 4$$
$$h^2 - 5h = 0$$
$$h(h-5) = 0$$

So $h = 0$ (original position) or $h = 5$. Taking $h = 5$:
$$k = 5 + 4 = 9$$

$$C = (8, 0)$$

Looking at this problem, I need to find the length of $AH$ given that the shell consists of six 30-60-90 triangles arranged in a fan around point $A$, with $AB = 1$ cm.

## Setting Up the Problem

Each triangle has:
- A 30° angle at vertex $A$
- A 90° angle at the other vertex
- The six 30° angles at $A$ total 180° (forming the straight line $HAB$)

**Key observation:** In each triangle, the side from $A$ serves as the hypotenuse for one triangle and as the leg for the next triangle.

## Analyzing Each Triangle

**Triangle $ABC$:** Right angle at $B$, so $AC$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AC = \frac{AB}{\cos 30°} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$$

**Triangle $ACD$:** Right angle at $D$, so $AC$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AD = AC \cdot \cos 30° = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 1$$

**Triangle $ADE$:** Right angle at $E$, so $AD$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AE = AD \cdot \cos 30° = 1 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

**Triangle $AEF$:** Right angle at $F$, so $AE$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AF = AE \cdot \cos 30° = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{3}{4}$$

**Triangle $AFG$:** Right angle at $G$, so $AF$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AG = AF \cdot \cos 30° = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}$$

**Triangle $AGH$:** Right angle at $G$, so $AG$ is the leg adjacent to $A$, and $AH$ is the hypotenuse.
$$AG = AH \cdot \cos 30°$$
$$AH = \frac{AG}{\cos 30°} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}/8}{\sqrt{3}/2} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$$

## Verification with Coordinates

Setting $A = (0,0)$ and working through the angles ($30°$ increments from 0° to 180°):
- $G = (-9/16, 3\sqrt{3}/16)$
- Computing $H$ on the line $y = 0$ with $GH \perp AG$ gives $H = (-3/4, 0)$

Therefore $AH = 3/4$ cm. ✓

So the final answer is $\boxed{\dfrac{3}{4}}$.

Looking at this problem, I need to find the angle ∠ACB in a right triangle where a specific perpendicular bisector condition holds.

## Setting Up Coordinates

Let me place the triangle in a coordinate system:
- $B = (0, 0)$
- $C = (c, 0)$  
- $A = (0, a)$ where $a = AB$ and $c = BC$

Since the right angle is at $B$, this gives $AB \perp BC$. ✓

## Locating Point T

**Finding M:** $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$, so $M = \left(\frac{c}{2}, 0\right)$

**Finding T:** Since $MT$ is perpendicular to $BC$, the line $MT$ is vertical, giving $T = \left(\frac{c}{2}, t\right)$ for some $t$.

Since $T$ lies on segment $AC$, I parametrize line $AC$ from $A(0,a)$ to $C(c,0)$:
$$\text{Point on } AC: \left(sc, a(1-s)\right) \text{ for } s \in [0,1]$$

Setting the x-coordinate equal to $\frac{c}{2}$:
$$sc = \frac{c}{2} \implies s = \frac{1}{2}$$

This means $T$ is the **midpoint of $AC$**, with coordinates $T = \left(\frac{c}{2}, \frac{a}{2}\right)$.

## Applying the Condition AT = AB

Computing $AT$:
$$AT = \sqrt{\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^2 + a^2}$$

Setting $AT = AB = a$:
$$\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^2 + a^2} = a$$
$$\sqrt{c^2 + a^2} = 2a$$
$$c^2 + a^2 = 4a^2$$
$$c^2 = 3a^2 \implies c = a\sqrt{3}$$

## Finding the Angle

In right triangle $ABC$ with right angle at $B$:
$$\tan(\angle ACB) = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{a}{a\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$$

Therefore:
$$\angle ACB = 30°$$

**Verification:** With $\angle ACB = 30°$, we get $\angle BAC = 60°$, so $AC = \frac{AB}{\cos 60°} = 2a$, and $T$ being the midpoint gives $AT = a = AB$. ✓

So the final answer is \boxed{30}.
