    $$

    $$

    $$
    y_V = -\frac{1}{4}(4+4)(4-12) = -\frac{1}{4}(8)(-8) = 16

To solve the problem, we use coordinate geometry to model the system, and use the property that the shortest segment between two parallel vertical lines is horizontal (perpendicular to both lines).
1.  **Set up the coordinate system**: Place the valley point $O$ at the origin $(0,0)$, with the horizontal axis as the $x$-axis and vertical axis as the $y$-axis.
2.  **Find coordinates of $A$ and $C$**:
    Point $A$ lies on the $30^\circ$ left hill, with $OA=20\ \text{m}$. The angle of $OA$ relative to the positive $x$-axis is $150^\circ$:

    A = \left(20\cos150^\circ, 20\sin150^\circ\right) = \left(-10\sqrt{3}, 10\right)
    $$
    Pole $AC$ is vertical, with length $6\ \text{m}$, so $C$ shares the $x$-coordinate of $A$, and its $y$-coordinate is increased by 6:
    $$

    $$

    $$
    D = \left(10\sqrt{2}, 10\sqrt{2} + h\right)
    $$

    $$

To solve the problem, we proceed step-by-step:
---
### Step 1: Define variables and use parallel line properties
Let $\angle ACB = \theta$, the angle we need to find.
Since $\triangle ABC$ is right-angled at $B$, $AB \perp BC$. We know $MT$ is the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, so $MT \perp BC$, and $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$.
Because both $AB$ and $MT$ are perpendicular to $BC$, **$AB \parallel MT$**. By the Basic Proportionality (Thales) theorem for similar triangles $\triangle CMT \sim \triangle CBA$:
$$
\frac{CM}{CB} = \frac{CT}{CA}
$$
Since $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$, $\frac{CM}{CB} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $\frac{CT}{CA} = \frac{1}{2}$. This means $T$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so:
$$
AT = TC = \frac{1}{2}AC
$$
---
### Step 2: Use the given condition $AT=AB$
Substitute the result above:
$$
AB = AT = \frac{1}{2}AC
$$
---
### Step 3: Solve for $\theta$
For right triangle $ABC$ (right-angled at $B$):
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite side to } \theta}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{AC}
$$
Substitute $AB = \frac{1}{2}AC$:
$$
\sin\theta = \frac{1}{2}
$$
Since $\theta$ is an acute angle in a right triangle, $\theta = 30^\circ$.
---
We can verify this with coordinate geometry: placing $B$ at $(0,0)$, $A$ at $(0,b)$, $C$ at $(c,0)$, we find $T = (c/2, b/2)$. Setting $AT = AB$ gives $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^2 + b^2} = b$, so $c = b\sqrt{3}$, and $\tan\theta = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, confirming $\theta=30^\circ$.
So the final answer is $\boxed{30}$.

### Step 1: Determine the equation of the circle
The circle has center $C(1,1)$ and passes through $O(0,0)$. The radius $r$ is:
$$
r = \sqrt{(1-0)^2 + (1-0)^2} = \sqrt{2}
$$
The equation of the circle is:
$$
(x-1)^2 + (y-1)^2 = 2
$$

---
### Step 2: Find coordinates of point $A$
Point $A$ is the intersection of the circle with the $y$-axis (other than $O(0,0)$). On the $y$-axis, $x=0$. Substitute $x=0$ into the circle equation:
$$
(0-1)^2 + (y-1)^2 = 2 \implies 1 + (y-1)^2 = 2 \implies (y-1)^2 = 1
$$
This gives $y=0$ (which is $O$) and $y=2$. Thus $A = (0,2)$.

---
### Step 3: Calculate the area of the circle in the first quadrant
1.  Notice that the circle passes through all four vertices of the square with vertices $(0,0), (0,2), (2,0), (2,2)$:
    - For $(2,2)$: $(2-1)^2 + (2-1)^2 = 2$, so it lies on the circle.
    - This square has side length 2, area $2\times 2 = 4$, and is entirely contained within the circle.
2.  The circle has 4 identical segments sticking out from each side of this square. For any side of the square (which is a chord of the circle):
    - The central angle for the chord is $90^\circ = \pi/2$ radians.
    - The area of the corresponding sector is $\frac{1}{4} \cdot \pi (\sqrt{2})^2 = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
    - The area of the triangle formed by the center and the chord is $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 1$.
    - The area of one segment is: sector area $-$ triangle area $= \frac{\pi}{2} - 1$.

    - The entire square of area 4 lies in the first quadrant.
    - Only 2 of the 4 segments (the right segment at $x>2$, and the top segment at $y>2$) lie in the first quadrant.

$$
