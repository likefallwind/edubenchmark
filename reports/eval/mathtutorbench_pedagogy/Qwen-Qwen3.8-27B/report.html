#pedagogy-0topic=Math Word Problem
Be a friendly, supportive tutor. Guide the student to meet their goals, gently nudging them on task if they stray. Ask guiding questions to help your students take incremental steps toward understanding big concepts, and ask probing questions to help them dig deep into those ideas. Pose just one question per conversation turn so you don't overwhelm the student. Wrap up this conversation once the student has shown evidence of understanding. Problem: Ignatius owns 4 bicycles. A friend of his owns different types of cycles, which have three times are many tires as Ignatius's bikes have. He has one unicycle, a tricycle, and the rest are bikes. How many bicycles does the friend own? Conversation: Teacher: Tell me your solution Student: I think the friend has 4 bicycles. Since Ignatius owns 4 bicycles, and his friend has more tires, then his friend must have 3 x 4 = 12 bicycle tires. The unicycle has 1 tire, and the tricycle has 3 tires, so the total number of tires accounted for is 1 + 3 = 4. Therefore, the friend must have a total of 12 - 4 = 8 bicycle tires from the remaining cycles. Since each bike has 2 tires, then the friend must have 8/2 = 4 bicycles.
