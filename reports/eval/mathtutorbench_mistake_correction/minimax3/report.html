# Solution

## Identifying the Student's Error

The student made a critical mistake by assuming that "every 15 minutes of charging adds 25%." This assumption is not supported by the problem. The problem actually tells us:
- 45 minutes of charging → 25% charge

The student then compounded this error by multiplying 45 minutes × 25%, which doesn't make sense (you can't multiply minutes by a percentage to get minutes).

## Correct Solution

**Step 1: Find the charging rate.**

We know that 45 minutes of charging adds 25% of battery.

$$\text{Rate} = \frac{25\%}{45 \text{ minutes}}$$

**Step 2: Determine how much more charge is needed.**

The phone is currently at 25%, and we need to reach 100%:

$$100\% - 25\% = 75\%$$

**Step 3: Calculate the additional time needed.**

Using a proportion, since 45 minutes provides 25% of charge, we need to find how many minutes provide 75%:

$$\frac{45 \text{ minutes}}{25\%} = \frac{x \text{ minutes}}{75\%}$$

Solving for $x$:

$$x = \frac{45 \times 75}{25} = 45 \times 3 = 135 \text{ minutes}$$

**Step 4: Verify the answer.**

In 135 additional minutes, the phone charges:
$$135 \times \frac{25\%}{45} = 3 \times 25\% = 75\% \checkmark$$

Adding this to the current 25% gives 100%. ✓

## Final Answer

**The cell phone must be charged for an additional 135 minutes to reach 100% charge.**

# Solution

This is an arithmetic sequence problem where:
- **Starting amount (first term):** 3 objects
- **Common difference:** 2 objects per week
- **Number of weeks:** 5

## Method 1: Step-by-Step (as the student did)

| Week | Calculation | Objects |
|------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Starting amount | 3 |
| 2 | 3 + 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 5 + 2 | 7 |
