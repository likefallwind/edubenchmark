Let \(x\) be the number of bedbugs the group started with.

Since the number triples every day, after 4 days the number is:

\[
3^4 x = 81x
\]

We are told there were 810 bedbugs after 4 days, so:

\[
81x = 810
\]

Divide both sides by 81:

\[
x = \frac{810}{81} = 10
\]

Check:

\[
10 \to 30 \to 90 \to 270 \to 810
\]

So the group started with 10 bedbugs.

Final Answer: 10

Heidi, you’re very close! Just be careful with how you state the relationship.

The problem says:

- Juan’s **lunch time** is **half** his **reading time**.
- The book has **4000 pages**.
- It takes him **4 hours** to grab lunch and return.

Let \(r\) be the number of hours Juan takes to read the book.

Since lunch time is half the reading time:

\[
\text{lunch time}=\frac{r}{2}
\]

We are told the lunch time is 4 hours:

\[
\frac{r}{2}=4
\]

\[
r=8
\]

\[
\frac{4000\text{ pages}}{8\text{ hours}}=500\text{ pages per hour}
\]

Check: If he reads 500 pages per hour, then

\[
500 \times 8 = 4000
\]

and half of 8 hours is 4 hours, which matches the lunch time.

Final Answer: 500 pages per hour

Good start, but let’s keep the cuts in order.

- Tom first cut the wood to **143 cm**.
- He then cut **25 cm** off:  
  \[
  143 - 25 = 118
  \]
  So after that cut, the board was **118 cm** long.

The **7 cm** cut happened **after** the board was already 118 cm long. So, before he cut off the 7 cm, the board was still **118 cm** long.

You do not add 7 here. Adding 7 would be correct if 118 cm were the length **after** the 7 cm cut. If we wanted the length **after** the 7 cm cut, it would be:
\[

\]
