Scott, thank you for walking me through your solution. You made a great start, but there are a couple of important details we need to look at more carefully. Let's read the problem again:

*"John's neighbor tells him to walk his dog for 1 hour each day **for a total of $10**."*

The key phrase here is **"a total of $10."** This means the neighbor agreed to pay John **$10 in total** for the entire job—not $10 per day. It's a one-time payment for walking the dog each day.  

The problem then says John does this for April, except on the 4 Sundays. That information about the days is extra and is meant to distract you. Since the payment is a flat $10 total, the number of days he walked does **not** change how much money he earned. He earned exactly **$10**.

Now let's calculate how much he spent:  
- Books: $50  
- Gave to his sister Kaylee: the same amount, so another $50  
- Total spent: $50 + $50 = **$100**

To find out how much money John has left, we subtract what he spent from what he earned:

Money earned − Money spent = $10 − $100 = **−$90**

John spent $90 more than he earned, so he is $90 in debt.

**Final Answer: −$90** (or "He is $90 in debt")

Jeanette starts with 3 objects and adds 2 more objects each week. This forms an arithmetic sequence where the first term is 3 and the common difference is 2.

We can list the number of objects she can juggle at the end of each week:

- Week 1: \(3\)
- Week 2: \(3 + 2 = 5\)
- Week 3: \(5 + 2 = 7\)
- Week 4: \(7 + 2 = 9\)
- Week 5: \(9 + 2 = 11\)

Alternatively, we can use the formula for the \(n\)-th term of an arithmetic sequence:  
\(a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d\), where \(a_1 = 3\), \(d = 2\), and \(n = 5\).

\(a_5 = 3 + (5-1) \times 2 = 3 + 4 \times 2 = 3 + 8 = 11\)

The student's approach has a common error: they divided the number of sold cones (50) by 6, but the free cones are given to every 6th customer overall, not every 6th paying customer. The free customers are mixed in with the paying ones, so we need to carefully count the sequence.

**Step 1: Find the number of cones sold**  
Each cone costs $2. Total sales = $100.  
Number of cones sold = $100 ÷ $2 = **50 cones**.

These 50 cones were bought by 50 paying customers. Free cones are not sold, so they are not included in this count.

**Step 2: Understand the customer sequence**  
Customers arrive in order: the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th pay; the 6th gets a free cone; the 7th, 8th, 9th, 10th, 11th pay; the 12th gets a free cone; and so on.

Free cones are given to customers at positions that are multiples of 6:  
6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60, …

**Step 3: Count how many free cones are given by the time 50 paying customers have been served**  
Each free cone is followed by 5 paying customers (except possibly at the end). Let's see how many paying customers we have after each free cone:

- After the 9th free cone (customer #54), total customers = 54.  
  Number of free cones = 9.  
  Number of paying customers = 54 − 9 = **45**.

We need 5 more paying customers to reach 50. These are customers #55, #56, #57, #58, and #59.  
So the 50th paying customer is customer #59. At this exact moment, the $100 total sales is reached.

The next free cone would be given to customer #60, but that customer has not arrived yet (we stop when the 50th sale is made).

Therefore, the number of free cones given away is **9**.

**Final Answer: 9**

Let's solve the problem step-by-step.

**Step 1: Define the variable.**  
Let \( t \) = the time (in hours) Juan takes to read the entire 4000-page book.

**Step 2: Translate the relationship.**  
The problem says: *"The time Juan takes to grab his lunch from his office and back is half the time he takes to read a book."*  
So, time to grab lunch = \( \frac{t}{2} \).

**Step 3: Use the given information.**  
We are told: *"he takes 4 hours to move from his office to grab lunch"*.  
This means the lunch-grabbing time is 4 hours.  
Set up the equation:  
\[
\frac{t}{2} = 4
\]

**Step 4: Solve for \( t \).**  
Multiply both sides by 2:  
\[
t = 8 \text{ hours}
\]  
So, Juan takes 8 hours to read the entire 4000-page book.

**Step 5: Find the reading speed (pages per hour).**  
Reading speed = \(\frac{\text{Total pages}}{\text{Total time}} = \frac{4000}{8} = 500\) pages per hour.

**Final Answer:** Juan reads **500 pages per hour**.
