The conditions allow for valid schedules where each day has exactly 3 batches, meaning no day is forced to have at most 2 batches. However, the question asks for days where it is possible to have at most 2 batches. For each day, a valid schedule can be constructed with 0, 1, or 2 batches on that day while satisfying all constraints. For example:

- Monday can have 1 batch (e.g., only O1) with other batches distributed appropriately.
- Tuesday can have 0 batches (e.g., no batches on Tuesday, with batches on other days).

归一化C
正确答案A
查看模型完整作答
Looking at this argument, I need to identify the logical flaw in the politician's reasoning.

**The politician's argument structure:**
1. Opponents argue for reducing social spending based on theoretical grounds.
2. The politician claims the main cause of deficit spending is bloated bureaucracy and self-aggrandizing politicians.
3. Therefore, reducing social expenditure is unwarranted.

**Analyzing the flaw:**

The politician's conclusion that reducing social spending is "unwarranted" only follows if bloated bureaucracy is the *only* relevant cause of deficit spending. However, deficit spending could have multiple contributing causes — including both bureaucratic bloat AND excessive social spending. The fact that bureaucracy is "the main cause" doesn't logically mean social spending isn't also contributing to the problem or shouldn't be addressed.

By identifying one main cause and using that to dismiss the opponents' position entirely, the politician assumes there is only one cause worth addressing. This is a single-cause fallacy — treating one cause as if it excludes all other possible causes.

**Checking other options:**
- (A) The politician does engage with opponents by redirecting to an alternative cause.
- (B) "Self-aggrandizing politicians" refers to those causing the deficit, not a personal attack on opponents.
- (D) The politician doesn't portray opponents' views as extreme; they simply disagree.
- (E) The argument isn't about defining "excessive" — it's about cause attribution.

The answer is C

Looking at this problem, I need to find when the ball hits the ground, which means finding when h = 0.

**Setting up the equation:**
$$-4.9t^2 + 25t = 0$$

**Factoring:**
$$t(-4.9t + 25) = 0$$

This gives t = 0 (initial launch) or:
$$t = \frac{25}{4.9} \approx 5.1 \text{ seconds}$$
